Independent contribution review, 10 October 2026. This is a read-only review of the existing contracts, native-check summaries, routed hardware records, restored-reference study, and primary prior-art texts. No simulation, route, power estimate, or frozen source was changed. Pending energy evidence is deliberately excluded from this verdict.

The current candidate has a defensible small-primitive contribution, but the evidence does not yet establish a strong ISCAS paper. Its plausible distinction is **exact ordered lane admission driven by the actual post-leak membrane**, over one invariant eight-field DSP residue layout. It consumes five through eight consecutive neurons, admitting each of the final three only when its actual U fits signed8. A rejected wide tail is deferred and subsequently handled by a full-range lane. The representation, field width, and DSP count do not change. Call this membrane-aware adaptive lane admission or adaptive useful packing capacity; avoid suggesting a variable-width DSP layout, floating-point rescaling, or an overflow spill scheduler.

The routed resource benefit is real but modest. At the matched 6 ns Artix7 fixture, F5 uses 108 LUTs, 328 FFs and one DSP. Compared with the full-range fixed8 precomputed-high design (118/364/1), that is 8.47% fewer LUTs and 9.89% fewer FFs. Compared with the fixed8 native-DSP/fabric hybrid (122/336/1), it is 11.48% fewer LUTs and 2.38% fewer FFs. The LUT-only implementation (98/404/0) uses ten fewer LUTs and no DSP, while F5 uses 76 fewer FFs. These are resource vectors, not a universal total-area ordering. F5 touches 77 core slices versus 70 for the hybrid, so LUT/FF reductions must not become a claim that every mapped-area metric improved. Same-clock setup and hold pass; a maximum-frequency advantage has not been measured.

Adaptation does not create a latency benefit over the strong fixed8 designs. The saved beta231 workload needs 119,870 F5 service clocks versus 119,808 fixed8, a 0.052% penalty. Against fixed6 it saves 18.71% service clocks, but fixed6 is substantially smaller at 45 LUTs/262 FFs/one DSP. The full-domain stress trace requires 115,049 F5 clocks versus 78,858 fixed8, a 45.89% penalty. That stress result is an exact adverse distribution, not evidence of representative SNN burst latency. Under the two-value supply control, every design needs the same 354,816 clocks. Eight-value delivery and the omitted memory/window hardware therefore remain material conditions of the favorable service result.

The restored references improve relevance without establishing broad generality. They preserve the saved weights, thresholds, leakage and reset, and reproduce all saved software states, spikes and predictions. They are three seeded 64-128-10 ANN-to-integer-SNN conversions on the same digits dataset. Leakage configurations are not independent model architectures, and the primitive is one post-leak U+I integration step. Leak, synaptic current generation, threshold/reset circuitry, state memory, and a complete accelerator are outside its hardware scope. Native replay of the new streams must be distinguished from their completed software verification; power on beta231 cannot be renamed power on the restored original references.

Shift1 has an indefinite signed8 post-leak certificate derived from each neuron's binary-spike current bounds. An eight-lane statically narrow exact implementation is therefore a legal stronger application baseline, and must be included before claiming an adaptive advantage for shift1. For shift3, global signed11 U is certified and F5/F6 software each add only four aggregate service clocks over fixed8 (119,812 versus 119,808). Some hidden neurons are also statically narrow, and the hidden layer has certified signed10 post-leak bounds across the three seeds: [-343,357], [-273,301], and [-329,336]. The generic full-U11 comparisons remain fair for their common declared domain; they do not establish superiority over every weight- or layer-specialized fixed design. If claiming best application-specific packing, this specialization needs a comparison or an explicit limitation.

Existing arithmetic identities are not the novelty. [DSP-Packing, Section VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.11028#S7) already describes independent SNN additions in a wide DSP adder with guard bits preventing cross-lane carries. [Arithmetic Packing, Sections III and V](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11065) develops guard/overflow strategies and identifies workload-driven dynamic packing as future work. These texts do not demonstrate this specific actual-membrane ordered prefix rule, but their absence of that rule is not proof of first-ever status. Fixed exact residue/high recovery is an implementation result and supporting baseline; a unique radix or lane count alone does not establish a strong standalone novelty claim.

Energy can strengthen the tradeoff only after all seven complete slow/max routed replays use the same accepted transport and zero pulse-error/rejection method, match native useful work, remain free of timing diagnostics and physical unknown activity, and pass the v4 provenance/annotation/interval audit. Earlier-method estimates must remain preliminary and excluded from final ordering. Any accepted value describes vendor-estimated dynamic energy of the common 290-FF measurement fixture, with device static power separate. It is not core-only, board, or complete-SNN energy. Small numerical differences need restrained interpretation; XPE serialization precision is not a model-accuracy confidence bound.

One wider-domain follow-up is scientifically justified. Original IF references violate U11, and their observed held-out range requires U12. More importantly, the saved weights certify post-integration-input U within [31L,31H] for all binary-spike sequences over 32 timesteps from zero, yielding the global post-leak range [-3100,2170], which genuinely needs U13. A matched U13/I8-to-U14 primitive trial would support those original references and test whether increased full-range metadata makes narrow-tail admission more useful. This is a finite-horizon safety justification, not arbitrary baseline widening or unbounded IF support. The already profiled IF F5 schedule totals 122,834 clocks versus 119,808 fixed8 (+2.526%); changing the full-range width does not change U8 admission or remove this penalty. The proposed 60 logical FF savings is not a measured mapped benefit. Finish the bounded U11 evidence first, then treat one matched wider-domain comparison as a relevant small-primitive extension, with the same exactness, fixture, fixed/hybrid/LUT and timing/energy gates.

The present outcome is **retain the candidate as a conditional resource/service tradeoff, without declaring publication strength or abandoning the question**. A stronger claim needs an audited energy/resource advantage that survives the strongest legal fixed controls and representative configurations, together with a clear admission safety/progress argument. Exactness protects accuracy; it is not an accuracy improvement over other exact baselines. Favorable narrow workloads demonstrate when the smaller tail recovery hardware pays off, while wide workloads expose the rescheduling cost. No FireFly implementation or complete-accelerator superiority has been reproduced here.
