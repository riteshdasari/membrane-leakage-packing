# Accepted GLIF dynamic-energy comparison: supporting explanation

`explain_glif_energy_v1.py` independently reproduced both certified strict audits identically, including all pinned source/artifact hashes, numerical-unchanged export certificates, physical activity mapping and exact pin replay. Both use the same expected-output NPZ SHA256 `07f68efe215e9e0dd8c70a009f97cca8c6a7cf8d3c7bc5de33062a4302b2d3eb` and bound cohort record:262,528 scalar leakage operations, signed16 domain,12ns clock,94 fixture FFs and25 C junction/typical process. Exact serialized voltage/current fractions reproduce the reported dynamic energy. Full receipts and component values are in `glif_energy_explanation_v1.json`.

E5 predicts21.599497 pJ/result versus E1's55.308385, a60.9472% reduction. Average dynamic power is3.328 versus8.614mW. E5 performs141,985 requests versus140,465, extending the audited ROI from1.685628 to1.703868ms (1.08209%). The energy advantage therefore survives a longer schedule; it does not arise from fewer requests.

The serialized XPE components associate29.3347 pJ/result of the33.7089 net saving with combinational logic and its signal activity, about87.0% of that saving. Clock-network savings contribute2.3056pJ; register logic and associated signals contribute2.9391pJ. This agrees with the smaller fabric network:62 versus202 LUTs,44 versus79 core FFs,1 versus33 reported CARRY4s. Both contain one DSP; its estimated energy is slightly higher for E5,5.4972 versus5.2072pJ/result, and both CEP signals are almost continuously enabled. This is a fabric/routing/clock-load trade-off rather than a DSP clock-gating gain. It remains an observational vendor-model decomposition, not an isolated causal experiment.

The XPE component sum has an explicit unassigned residual; its contribution to the E1-minus-E5 difference is−0.5805pJ/result. No unexplained residual is silently assigned to a component. Reports serialize components to six decimal places; report-serialization bounds describe rounding only and do not bound physical or vendor-model error. Coarse hierarchy reports are consistent with core power near2mW versus8mW, but do not permit an exact separate common-fixture power claim. Identical fixture FF count does not imply identical switching or clock loading.
